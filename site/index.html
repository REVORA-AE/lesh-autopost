Reel 005

Why do buildings in Dubai have a 10 digit number?

Posted 28 Nov 2026. Sources checked 27 Nov 2026.

Watch the reel

The 7 facts in this reel, and where each one comes from

  1. Buildings carry a 10 digit number on a plaque at the entrance.

    • Gulf News, Makani number: what is it and why do you need it?
    • Bayut, Makani number in Dubai

  2. It is called Makani, which means "my location" in Arabic.

    • Gulf News, Makani number: what is it and why do you need it?

  3. The number comes from a geographic coordinate standard and finds the entrance to within about one metre.

    • Gulf News, Makani number: what is it and why do you need it?
    • The National, New geographic address system launched in Dubai

  4. Big buildings get a different number for each entrance.

    • Emirates 24/7 (WAM), Dubai's e address system begins in October (Aug 2015)
    • Dubai Municipality, Makani public web service access (PDF)

  5. You can share the number instead of describing the way.

    • Gulf News, Makani to cut response time in Dubai emergencies

  6. Taxis can use it, and it helps emergency teams reach you faster.

    • Gulf News, Makani to be usable on Google and HERE maps
    • Gulf News, Makani to cut response time in Dubai emergencies

  7. Ajman, Fujairah, Ras Al Khaimah and Umm Al Quwain use it too.

    • Dubai Municipality, GeoDubai smart solutions: Makani
    • Emirates 24/7, Makani explained

Every number in the reel is made up (12345 67890 and the two on the building), and the square code on the plaque is a pattern, not a readable code. The plaque, building, map and vehicles are generic drawings.

Reel 002

Where does Dubai's tap water come from?

Posted 27 Nov 2026. Sources checked 21 Nov 2026.

Watch the reel

The 7 facts in this reel, and where each one comes from

  1. Dubai's tap water starts as seawater with the salt taken out.

    • UAE Government portal u.ae, Water

  2. One way is to boil it: heat the seawater, the steam rises, the salt stays behind, and the cooled steam is fresh water.

    • Membranes journal via PubMed Central, seawater reverse osmosis review (2021)

  3. The other way is to squeeze it: pumps push seawater against a membrane at around 60 times normal air pressure.

    • Membranes journal via PubMed Central, seawater reverse osmosis review (2021)
    • IEEE TechNav, Reverse osmosis

    The review gives 5 to 7 megapascals, which is about 50 to 70 times normal air pressure.

  4. Water gets through the membrane. Over 99% of the salt does not.

    • IEEE TechNav, Reverse osmosis

  5. The salty leftover goes back to the sea.

    • WateReuse Association, Seawater Concentrate Management (white paper)
    • Science Times, How desalination plants use reverse osmosis and manage brine (Apr 2026)

  6. Squeezing takes less energy than boiling.

    • USGS, Reverse osmosis desalination

  7. Minerals go back in, because very pure water tastes flat and is hard on pipes.

    • Omya, Why remineralization makes drinking water safe
    • World Health Organization, Safe Drinking-water from Desalination (2011)

This reel explains the science only. Every drawing is a generic diagram, not a real place.

Reel 004

Can a number plate cost more than the car?

Posted 25 Nov 2026. Sources checked 24 Nov 2026.

Watch the reel

The 4 facts in this reel, and where each one comes from

  1. Plate P 7 sold for AED 55 million at an auction in Dubai on 8 April 2023.

    • Khaleej Times, New record set as car number plate P7 sells for Dh55 million (8 Apr 2023)
    • Guinness World Records, Dubai driver drops nearly $15 million on most expensive license plate ever (May 2023)

  2. P 7 has one letter and one digit. The fewer the digits, the rarer and more expensive the plate.

    • Khaleej Times, Dh55 million for a car number plate? Why they cost so much

  3. Guinness World Records lists it as the most expensive number plate ever sold.

    • Guinness World Records, Dubai driver drops nearly $15 million on most expensive license plate ever (May 2023)

  4. It was a charity auction. All the money went to a food aid fund, the 1 Billion Meals Endowment.

    • Khaleej Times, How UAE set a world record to feed the hungry (Apr 2023)
    • Khaleej Times, New record set as car number plate P7 sells for Dh55 million (8 Apr 2023)

Argument 01. Akeed's line (a plate can't cost more than the car) is the myth being corrected. The car, the plates and the rosette in the reel are toy models, not copies of real designs; 12345, 8642 and 371 are made up numbers.

Reel 003

What's holding up Palm Jumeirah?

Posted 23 Nov 2026. Sources checked 21 Nov 2026.

Watch the reel

The 10 facts in this reel, and where each one comes from

  1. The island is built only from sand and rock. No steel, no concrete.

    • Royal IHC, Creating the Palm Islands
    • Gulf News, 20 years since construction began on Palm Jumeirah
    • Property Finder, Palm Jumeirah facts

  2. The breakwater was built first.

    • Higgins, Engineering Challenges of Dubai's Palm Jumeirah, Memorial University (2013)
    • University of British Columbia, sand mining in Dubai case study
    • USC Illumin, The artificial natural world

  3. The crescent breakwater is 11 km long.

    • Gulf News, Palm Jumeirah to leave an indelible impression
    • Interesting Engineering

  4. Over 7 million tonnes of rock came from the Hajar Mountains.

    • Gulf News, 20 years since construction began on Palm Jumeirah
    • Gulf News, Palm Jumeirah to leave an indelible impression

  5. Each rock was placed by crane, signed off by a diver and given a GPS coordinate.

    • Gulf News, Palm Jumeirah to leave an indelible impression

  6. Sand was dredged from the seabed and sprayed into place. The method is called rainbowing.

    • Higgins, Engineering Challenges of Dubai's Palm Jumeirah, Memorial University (2013)
    • Gulf News, Palm Jumeirah to leave an indelible impression
    • Dubizzle, How Dubai's artificial islands were made

  7. About 94 million cubic metres of sand.

    • University of British Columbia, sand mining in Dubai case study
    • Property Finder, Palm Jumeirah facts
    • Dubizzle, How Dubai's artificial islands were made

    Sources differ here. Gulf News gives 95 million, and some give up to 110 to 120 million. We say about 94 million.

  8. GPS guided the spraying.

    • Higgins, Engineering Challenges of Dubai's Palm Jumeirah, Memorial University (2013)
    • Dubizzle, How Dubai's artificial islands were made

  9. Loose wet sand can act like a liquid when shaken, so the sand was vibrated tight.

    • Arabian Business, Sand compaction still underway on the Palm
    • USC Illumin, The artificial natural world

  10. Two 100 m gaps in the crescent let seawater circulate.

    • USC Illumin, The artificial natural world
    • Higgins, Engineering Challenges of Dubai's Palm Jumeirah, Memorial University (2013)
    • Property Finder, Palm Jumeirah facts

Reel 001

Who's driving the Dubai Metro?

Posted 22 Nov 2026. Sources checked 21 Nov 2026.

Watch the reel

The 8 facts in this reel, and where each one comes from

  1. There is no driver on board. Computers run every train.

    • Gulf News, Inside Dubai Metro's nerve centre

  2. Trains talk to the track all the time, and the system sets their speed and position.

    • Gulf News, How technology powers Dubai's transport evolution

  3. Each train keeps a moving safe zone of empty track around it.

    • Railway News, What is CBTC?
    • Wikipedia, Communications based train control

  4. Stations have glass doors along the platform edge.

    • Wikipedia, Dubai Metro

  5. A control centre watches the network day and night.

    • Gulf News, Dubai Metro: How 9,000 cameras track 850,000 riders (2025)

  6. 99.7% of trips run on time.

    • Gulf News, Dubai Metro: How 9,000 cameras track 850,000 riders (2025)

  7. In 2011 the Red Line set the Guinness record for the longest driverless metro line: 52.1 km.

    • Guinness World Records, Longest driverless metro line

  8. Over 750,000 riders on an average day.

    • Dubai Media Office, Dubai Metro marks 15 years (8 Sep 2024)

The KM readout, the train number, the speed bars and the map in the reel are drawings, not data.
